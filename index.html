Settings & accessibility

Controls
Keyboard and gamepad controls
ActionKeyboardGamepad

Gamepads work out of the box: stick or d-pad to move, A to jump, X or B to dash, Start to pause, Y to read out your status. Buttons are named for an Xbox-style pad.

Assist mode

Fell Runner is meant to be challenging, but everyone should be able to reach the summit. Assisted times are kept on their own leaderboard, so feel free.

Visuals
Sound

Press I (or Y on a gamepad) during a run to hear your time, where you are, the wind, and your dashes.

Data

Best times and ghosts are stored only in this browser.

How to play

Run from the valley to the summit trig point as fast as you can. The clock starts on your first move.

Moving on the fells

  • Jump higher by holding the button. You can still jump just after running off an edge, and a press just before landing is remembered.
  • Crouch by holding down on the ground. You slide to a stop. You can jump, or dash sideways, and stay crouched, which gets you through gaps too low to stand in. You stand up again as you start to fall, if there's room.
  • Cling to a crag just by touching it in mid-air: your grip never tires. Hold up to climb, slowly, and over the top; hold down to slide; push away to let go. Beside a crag on the ground, hold up to start climbing.
  • Wall jump by pressing jump on a crag or just beside one. You always kick away from the face.
  • Dash in any of eight directions. Your buff turns orange when a dash is ready and blue once it's spent. Touch the ground to recharge.
  • Kendal mint cake refills your dash in mid-air. On Scafell Pike, some gaps can only be crossed by dashing to a mint cake and dashing again.
  • Loose rock (blocky brown slate) cracks, with a sharp click, as soon as you stand on it or cling to it, and falls away a moment later. Keep moving. It settles back after a couple of seconds.
  • Cairns are checkpoints. Sharp rocks, tarns and big drops send you back to the last one.
  • Signposts on the way up Blencathra explain each move as you pass them. You can turn the tips off in Settings.

Weather

  • Wind is a force: it pushes you around, building speed as it goes. On the ground your feet hold you, and in the air you can push back, but the strongest gusts hold you still in mid-air however hard you push. Clinging to a crag, it can't move you at all. It's gentler down in the valley and full strength on the tops. Watch the wind gauge, streaks and your buff. The gust warning gives about a second's notice.
  • Shelter: crags, boulders and walls on the windward side break the wind. The gauge says Sheltered when you're in their lee. Crouch to get right down behind low rocks.
  • Rain and snow show the wind: the harder it blows, the more they fly sideways, and in the strongest wind they don't fall at all.
  • Clag (hill fog) hides the way ahead. You can limit it in Settings.

Speedrunning

  • Every run's weather comes from its seed. The same seed gives the same gusts at the same moments, so share one with friends for a fair race.
  • Each fell has target times: gold for a skilled runner's pace, then silver and bronze. They're the same in every weather, so a storm makes them harder.
  • After a summit, the restart key or button (R, or Back on a gamepad) runs it again, just as it restarts a run.
  • Your fastest run on each fell and weather setting is saved with a ghost to race. Splits at each cairn show whether you're ahead, and the route map at the bottom of the screen shows you (●) and your ghost (○).
  • Watch best run replays your ghost in the weather it ran in. You can also watch any run again from the results screen.
  • Dash jump: jump during a dash along the ground, and you carry the dash's speed into the air. Crouch first, and you stay low.

Real fells & mountain rescue

Sharp Edge, Striding Edge and the Scafell massif are real places, and real people are hurt on them every year, often in exactly the weather this game throws at you. On the real fells there are no respawns.

  • Check a mountain forecast before you go, such as the Lake District Weatherline (opens in a new tab) or MWIS (opens in a new tab).
  • Carry a map and compass and know how to use them, plus warm layers, a waterproof, a torch and food.
  • Turning back is always an option.

In an emergency in the UK mountains, dial 999, ask for the Police, then Mountain Rescue.

Lakeland's mountain rescue teams are unpaid volunteers, on call around the clock, every day of the year, and funded mostly by donations. If you enjoy the fells, in real life or in pixels, please consider supporting them.
